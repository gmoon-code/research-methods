# First-Contact Novice Usability Protocol v2.13.2

## Session purpose

Test whether a person with little or no formal research-methods experience can understand early research decisions, find help, and recover from uncertainty without the observer supplying the research answer.

Recommended duration is 35–45 minutes.

## Before the participant begins

Confirm the approved privacy and consent/assent procedure for the intended use. Use participant codes when names are unnecessary.

If the activity will produce research data about students, follow the applicable institutional, school, parental-consent, assent, and ethics requirements before collection.

## Observer introduction

Read this without adding research instruction.

> You are testing a research-planning website. I am interested in where the website is clear and where it becomes confusing. You are not being graded. Please use the website the way you normally would. You may use any help built into the site. If something is confusing, say what you are thinking. I may stay quiet for a while because I want to see whether the website itself helps.

Do not tell the participant which pathway, field, design, or statistical method to choose.

## Task 1 — begin a project

Ask the participant to create a project, enter a short project name, begin Stage 1, and explain what they think the first stage wants them to do.

Record time from page load to first meaningful action, whether Stage Guide is noticed, whether Progressive Help is noticed, and any terminology clarification.

## Task 2 — develop a researchable question

The participant may use their own safe topic or one supplied scenario card.

Ask them to work through Stages 1–4 until they can explain the topic/phenomenon, context/population/system, evidence they would need, current research question, and why it seems feasible.

Do not evaluate scientific quality during the session unless the application itself identifies a blocker.

## Task 3 — choose or confirm a research path

Ask

> Which research path does the website currently recommend or help you consider, and why do you think it fits?

Record whether the participant can distinguish in their own words among describing, observing an association, assigning an intervention, comparing a nonrandom intervention, studying experiences/meanings, reviewing literature, pooling quantitative effects, and combining quantitative with qualitative evidence.

Exact vocabulary is not required if the reasoning is correct.

## Task 4 — deliberate uncertainty

Choose one field the participant has not completed.

Ask

> Imagine you are completely unsure how to answer this field. Show me what you would do.

Observe whether the participant finds field-level Progressive Help, global I’m stuck, Research Terms, local review, or the path-matched exemplar. If a worked exemplar opens, confirm that the Level 4 warning is visible and understood.

## Task 5 — save/recovery awareness

Ask

> If you had to leave now and were worried about losing your work, what would you do?

The participant should be able to locate Pilot & Recovery controls or explain that they would create a backup. They do not need to know browser-storage terminology.

## Post-task questions

Ask in this order.

1. What was the first point where you were unsure what to do?
2. Was there any research word you still do not understand?
3. When you were stuck, what help did you expect the site to provide?
4. Was any explanation too long, too short, or too technical?
5. Did any example make you more confused about your own project?
6. Did you ever feel that the site was choosing the answer for you?
7. If you returned tomorrow, what would you expect the site to remember?
8. What is one thing you would change before another beginner used it?

## Completion categories

- completed without observer help
- completed after built-in help
- completed after neutral observer prompt
- completed after task clarification
- completed after external concept rescue
- not completed

Keep these categories separate from the application's scaffold levels.
