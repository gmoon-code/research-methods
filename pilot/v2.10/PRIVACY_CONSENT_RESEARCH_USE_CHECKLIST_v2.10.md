# Privacy, Consent, and Research-Use Decision Checklist v2.10

This checklist does not replace institutional, legal, or school review.

## Decide what the activity is

### Internal instructional improvement only

The school may still have privacy, parental communication, record-retention, or approval requirements.

### Research involving students or student records

Before collection, determine whether institutional review, school approval, parental consent, student assent, or other authorization is required.

Do not assume a normal classroom activity automatically permits using the resulting records for scholarship, publication, conference presentation, or doctoral-application research.

## Data minimization

Prefer participant codes.

Do not put student names into usability-event logs when codes are sufficient.

Do not collect passwords, credentials, unnecessary health information, disability information, family financial information, or unrelated educational records.

If demographics are required for an approved research question, collect only justified variables.

## Screen/audio/video recording

Do not record merely because it is convenient.

Obtain required approval and consent/assent first. If recording is not approved, use timestamped observer notes.

## Teacher-student power relationship

When participation is voluntary, make clear that grades do not depend on participation, declining does not affect access to instruction, and recruitment/consent should be separated from grading where required.

## Storage

Document where files are stored, who can access them, how participant codes are linked if at all, how long raw records are retained, when identifiable data are destroyed/de-identified, and how backups are protected.

## Before first participant

The pilot coordinator should be able to answer:

1. What exact data are being collected?
2. Why is each field necessary?
3. Who can see the raw records?
4. Is participation required or voluntary?
5. Does a participant need parental consent/assent?
6. Is this internal improvement, research, or both?
7. What happens if a participant asks to stop?
8. What happens to data after withdrawal?
9. What is the retention/deletion plan?
10. Has required school/institutional approval been obtained?
