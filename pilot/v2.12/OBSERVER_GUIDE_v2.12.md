# Observer Guide v2.12

## Primary rule

Record what happened before interpreting why it happened.

Prefer

> 14:08 participant reopened Research Terms three times and said, “I still don't know what binary means.”

Avoid

> Participant lacks statistical ability.

## Event codes

`NAV` navigation confusion

`TERM` terminology confusion

`TASK` misunderstanding of the requested task

`PATH` research-path confusion

`METHOD` design / variable / unit / sampling misconception

`STAT` statistical reasoning misconception

`SOURCE` search / source-evaluation problem

`WRITE` writing / section-boundary problem

`HELP` built-in help could not resolve the problem

`EXAMPLE` exemplar caused confusion or copying attempt

`SAVE` saving / backup / restore problem

`A11Y` keyboard, readability, focus, touch, contrast, or accessibility problem

`TEACHER` teacher-review workflow problem

`PRIVACY` privacy / consent / data-handling concern

## Severity

### Blocker

The participant cannot continue, work can be lost/corrupted, privacy is compromised, or the software permits a materially invalid action with no effective warning.

### Major

A common task is substantially impaired or a novice can reach a seriously misleading methodological conclusion despite using available guidance.

### Moderate

The task remains possible, but unnecessary confusion, extra steps, or misleading wording creates meaningful friction.

### Minor

Cosmetic or low-impact issue that does not meaningfully change completion or research reasoning.

## What to timestamp

At minimum record task start, first meaningful action, first help request, first built-in scaffold use, first observer prompt, completion/abandonment, and backup/export start/finish when tested.

## Note structure

Keep separate columns for observed behavior, probable cause, evidence for interpretation, and possible fix.

Do not modify software during the session to test a fix.

## Think-aloud handling

Participants may stop talking while thinking. Do not repeatedly prompt narration.

If needed, ask

> What are you looking for right now?

Avoid leading wording such as

> Are you confused about the experimental unit?
