# Multi-Path Exemplar Library v2.8

## Purpose

v2.7 provided one complete experimental exemplar. That solved the need for an end-to-end model but still risked making experimental research look like the default form of rigorous research.

v2.8 gives every supported confirmed research pathway its own complete 18-stage worked exemplar.

## Eight exemplar pathways

1. Descriptive quantitative
2. Observational / correlational
3. Experimental
4. Quasi-experimental
5. Qualitative
6. Literature review
7. Meta-analysis
8. Mixed methods

The `Unsure` pathway does not pretend to be a ninth research design. Students who are still deciding can browse the entire library.

## Automatic path matching

The Stage-level **Worked Stage** control now opens the exemplar matching the student's current confirmed research path.

Examples

- Observational student → sleep/quiz association exemplar
- Qualitative student → multilingual biology explanation interview exemplar
- Literature-review student → multilingual science mini-review
- Meta-analysis student → synthetic effect-synthesis exemplar

Students can deliberately inspect a different path, but the interface labels it as a cross-path comparison and warns them not to transfer incompatible terminology, unit logic, analysis, or causal claims.

## Every exemplar has 18 worked stages

Every stage contains

- where the exemplar is now
- what changed
- reasoning sequence
- decision kept
- tempting alternative rejected
- notebook content after the decision
- how the decision constrains the next stage
- comparison prompts for the student's own work

Total worked-stage records

`8 pathways × 18 stages = 144`

## Design-specific instructional threads

### Descriptive quantitative

No forced hypothesis, treatment, IV/DV, or p-value. The exemplar teaches observation units, repeated monitoring, distributions, and descriptive claim boundaries.

### Observational

Measured predictor/exposure, confounding, convenience sampling, association estimates, and explicit noncausal language.

### Experimental

Treatment assignment, experimental unit, subsamples, pseudoreplication prevention, and treatment-effect claims within experimental scope.

### Quasi-experimental

Pre-existing groups, baseline information, nonrandom assignment, comparative change, selection/confounding threats, and restricted causal interpretation.

### Qualitative

Phenomenon, purposive cases, interview evidence, coding, themes, reflexivity, credibility, discrepant evidence, and transferability.

### Literature review

Unit of inclusion, source roles, search/selection records, study matrix, thematic/conceptual synthesis, and coverage limits without invented pooled statistics.

### Meta-analysis

Independent study/effect units, effect metric, uncertainty, dependence, random-effects pooling, heterogeneity, and the distinction between p-values and effect sizes.

### Mixed methods

Separate quantitative and qualitative strands, strand-specific analysis, explicit integration, joint interpretation, and the rule that combining two strands does not automatically create causal evidence.

## Evidence status

The library distinguishes three evidence types.

### Real verified source records

The literature and methods references are real and verified.

### Synthetic project evidence

Numerical student records, interview excerpts, effect estimates, class scores, aquarium measurements, and experimental observations are invented teaching data where indicated.

### Verified-source mini review

The literature-review exemplar uses a small real source corpus. It is explicitly a mini review for teaching and is not presented as exhaustive or systematic field coverage.

## Scaffold accounting

Viewing any complete worked stage records Level 4 support for that stage.

Viewing a second exemplar for the same stage records another worked-example exposure. This preserves the fact that the student consulted multiple examples.

The earlier eligible independent checkpoint remains preserved if it was saved before exemplar exposure.

## No copying into the project

No exemplar includes a copy/apply-to-project action. Students can save comparison notes, then return to their own pathway-specific notebook.

## Baseline consequence

The exemplar condition changes substantially from v2.7 because nonexperimental students now receive a methodology-matched complete worked example.

Use `RMS-INSTRUCTIONAL-BASELINE-v2.8`.
