# Research Methods Studio v1.0 — Instructional Specification

## Product purpose

Teach students how to perform research and write a research paper by making the reasoning process visible and scaffolded.

The system is not primarily a paper generator. Its central unit is a student research decision.

## Learning architecture

Every stage uses:

1. Learn the concept
2. See a worked example and common failure modes
3. Do the student's own research work
4. Run checks or receive structured feedback
5. Revise
6. Mark the stage ready
7. Preserve the notebook for later alignment

## Five phases and 18 stages

### Phase 1 — Discover
1. Find research interests
2. Narrow a topic and test feasibility
3. Explore the research landscape
4. Write and refine the research question

### Phase 2 — Build the evidence base
5. Build a systematic search strategy
6. Evaluate, read, and extract sources
7. Synthesize the literature and justify the study
8. Write the literature review

### Phase 3 — Design the study
9. Choose the research design
10. Define hypotheses, variables, controls, and experimental units
11. Plan sampling, measurement, validity, and ethics
12. Write the method and lock the data plan

### Phase 4 — Collect and analyze
13. Collect, clean, visualize, and describe data
14. Choose and justify the data analysis
15. Write the Results section

### Phase 5 — Write and audit
16. Write the Discussion and limitations
17. Write the conclusion, abstract, title, and keywords
18. Citations, references, and final alignment audit

## Scientific-method corrections built into v1.0

The site deliberately avoids several common oversimplifications.

- Research is not a single fixed linear “scientific method.”
- Not every study needs an independent variable, dependent variable, or hypothesis.
- A predictor/exposure in observational research is distinguished from a manipulated independent variable.
- Control group/condition is distinguished from controlled conditions.
- Experimental unit is explicit to reduce pseudoreplication.
- Statistical tests are selected from design and data structure, not from a generic normal/non-normal branch.
- Welch's t-test is presented as a robust default for two independent means when appropriate.
- Normality assumptions are described at the level relevant to the model, such as paired difference scores or residuals.
- p-values are not treated as effect sizes, practical importance, or the probability a hypothesis is true.
- non-significance is not treated as proof of no effect.
- magnitude, uncertainty, design limitations, and claim scope are all required for interpretation.
- Literature “gaps” must be evidenced. Replication, extension, local application, and learning-focused inquiry are valid rationales without invented novelty.
- Citation integrity is separated from citation formatting.

## Future research/AI layer

A later AI-assisted version should diagnose student reasoning without replacing it. It should preserve first attempts, cite retrieved sources, refuse to fabricate references or data, and route methodology/statistics through validated rules.
