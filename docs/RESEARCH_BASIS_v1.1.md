# Research Basis for v1.1

The instructional architecture uses the following principles.

1. Science learning should engage students in asking questions, planning investigations, analyzing/interpreting data, constructing explanations, arguing from evidence, and communicating information. These practices are iterative and used in combination.
2. Statistical investigation begins with a question answerable with data, followed by data collection, analysis, and interpretation in context. Variability is central throughout.
3. Literature-review instruction should move students from source-by-source summary toward synthesis, comparison, methodological evaluation, and evidence-based justification.
4. Research writing is treated as a connected evidence chain. Research question, design, measurement, data structure, analysis, results, and conclusion must align.
5. Guidance is progressively diagnostic. Students should make epistemically important decisions before receiving direct support.

This file is a design rationale, not a formal systematic review.
