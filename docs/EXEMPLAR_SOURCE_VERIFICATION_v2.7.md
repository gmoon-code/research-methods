# Exemplar Source Verification v2.7

The worked exemplar uses three real literature records. The exemplar's experimental data remain synthetic.

## Verified records

Munns, R., & Tester, M. (2008). Mechanisms of salinity tolerance. *Annual Review of Plant Biology, 59*, 651–681. https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev.arplant.59.032607.092911

Verification basis: PubMed bibliographic record and DOI.

Use in exemplar: broad mechanisms and terminology for plant salinity stress.

Boundary: this review does not provide the numerical effect for the synthetic classroom protocol.

Munns, R., & Gilliham, M. (2015). Salinity tolerance of crops – what is the cost? *New Phytologist, 208*(3), 668–673. https://doi.org/10.1111/nph.13519

Verification basis: Wiley / New Phytologist and PubMed bibliographic records.

Use in exemplar: broad evidence that salinity can constrain crop growth and yield.

Boundary: it does not estimate the effect for the particular radish seed batch represented by the synthetic data.

Toscano, S., Romano, D., Cafaro, V., & Patanè, C. (2025). Annual Garden Rocket and Radish as Microgreens: Seed Germination Response to Thermal and Salt Stress. *Agronomy, 15*(2), 361. https://doi.org/10.3390/agronomy15020361

Verification basis: publisher record.

Use in exemplar: species-relevant evidence that radish germination and early radicle growth respond to saline germination conditions.

Boundary: its temperature, water-potential/salinity treatments, and outcome procedures differ from the synthetic exemplar.

## Synthetic-data rule

None of the following are presented as observed or published findings:

- the 90 seed-level records
- the 18 dish-level records
- the Welch ANOVA result
- the 0 versus 100 mM contrast
- the exemplar's classroom protocol

They exist only to demonstrate the research-methods workflow.
