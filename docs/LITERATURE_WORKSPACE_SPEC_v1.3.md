# Literature Research Workspace v1.3

## Purpose

Teach students the intermediate reasoning between finding papers and writing a literature review.

The workspace operationalizes the course sequence:

search → evaluate → screen → extract → synthesize → justify → trace claims → outline.

## Source-derived foundations

The uploaded course materials emphasize:
- TRAPP evaluation using Timeframe, Relevance, Authority, Accuracy, and Purpose
- a study summary/source evaluation/synthesis matrix workflow
- avoiding serial article summaries
- literature organization by theme, perspective, chronology, methodology, or theory
- a required study matrix
- theme synthesis tables with supporting and conflicting studies
- evidence mapping by outcome, measure, population/context, and design
- explicit gap/inconsistency statements
- no pooled statistics in a conventional literature review

## Workspace tabs

1. Search log
2. Sources & TRAPP
3. Screening
4. Study matrix
5. Synthesis matrix and evidence map
6. Gap/study-rationale evidence check
7. Claim-source traceability
8. Literature-review paragraph planner

## Important methodological refinement

The system does not equate “I did not find a paper” with a global research gap.

Gap language is calibrated to:
- search coverage
- number of included studies
- repeated limitations
- multi-source themes
- conflicting or mixed evidence
- diversity of measures/contexts

Replication, extension, method comparison, inconsistent findings, local questions, and learning-focused investigations remain valid rationales.

## Student authorship

The paragraph planner does not generate finished prose. Students specify:
- paragraph job
- literature claim
- sources
- synthesis/comparison
- contradiction/qualification
- method/context explanation
- transition/implication

This preserves the intellectual work of synthesis.
